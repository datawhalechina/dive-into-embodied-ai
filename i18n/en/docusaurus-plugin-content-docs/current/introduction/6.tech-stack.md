# 6. Technology stack

Embodied AI spans a broad set of technologies, from model training to robot hardware. This chapter introduces eight technical areas: the problems they solve, their core technologies, and the roles associated with them. A real project usually draws on several areas.

## 6.1 Foundation model algorithms \{#foundation-models}

Foundation model algorithms help robots understand images, language, and tasks, then generate actions or predict changes in the environment. Work includes model design, pretraining, fine-tuning, post-training, and evaluation.

**Core technologies**:

- **Multimodal modeling**: Transformers, vision-language models (VLMs), vision-language-action models (VLAs), and world models for prediction and planning.
- **Action generation**: discrete action representations, diffusion models, and flow matching. [OpenVLA](https://openvla.github.io/), [RDT](https://github.com/thu-ml/RoboticsDiffusionTransformer), and [π0](/docs/foundations/vla/pi0_foundation_model) illustrate different ways to represent and generate actions.
- **Training and post-training**: PyTorch / JAX, FSDP / DeepSpeed, multi-GPU training, data mixtures, cross-embodiment training, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning post-training using interaction feedback.

**Related roles**: embodied foundation model engineer, VLA engineer, multimodal ML engineer, world model engineer, and reinforcement learning roles focused on robot policy post-training.

On this site: [VLA overview](/docs/foundations/vla/vla-intro) · [OpenVLA engineering](/docs/foundations/vla/openvla_engineering) · [π0.6 online learning](/docs/foundations/vla/pi06_online_learning).

## 6.2 Manipulation algorithms \{#manipulation}

Manipulation algorithms enable robot arms, bimanual systems, and dexterous hands to grasp, transport, assemble, and open doors. They connect object perception, motion planning, policy learning, and contact control.

**Core technologies**:

- **Object and grasp perception**: point cloud processing, segmentation, 6D pose estimation, and grasping work such as GraspNet / AnyGrasp.
- **Motion planning and control**: forward and inverse kinematics, trajectory planning, collision detection, [MoveIt 2](https://moveit.picknik.ai/main/index.html), impedance control, and force control.
- **Learning and dexterous manipulation**: ACT, Diffusion Policy, imitation learning, bimanual coordination, and dexterous-hand motion retargeting, which maps motions from a human hand or another embodiment to the target hand's joints and constraints.

**Related roles**: manipulation engineer, grasping engineer, robot arm algorithm engineer, and dexterous manipulation engineer. Work focused on object recognition and pose estimation also maps to perception / 3D vision roles.

On this site: [ACT bimanual training](/docs/practices/vla/act) · [Diffusion Policy](/docs/foundations/vla/diffusion_policy) · [Impedance and force control](/docs/foundations/controllers/impedance-force-control).

## 6.3 Motion control algorithms \{#motion-control}

Motion control algorithms handle balance, walking, velocity tracking, and whole-body coordination. They make actions execute reliably within joint, torque, contact, and real-time computation constraints. Common approaches are learning-based and model-based control, which can also be combined.

**Core technologies**:

- **Learning-based control**: [Isaac Lab](https://isaac-sim.github.io/IsaacLab/main/index.html), MuJoCo, PPO, reward design, curriculum learning, domain randomization, motion imitation, and sim-to-real transfer.
- **Model-based control**: rigid-body dynamics, model predictive control (MPC), whole-body control (WBC), and numerical optimization, with tools such as Pinocchio, [OCS2](https://leggedrobotics.github.io/ocs2/), and CasADi.
- **Hardware execution**: state estimation, actuator modeling, control rates and timing, C++ / Python implementation, and handling delays, noise, and model errors.

**Related roles**: motion control engineer, reinforcement learning locomotion engineer, legged robot controls engineer, and humanoid whole-body control engineer. RL here mainly optimizes motion and body coordination; VLA RL post-training focuses on task policies, with different training and deployment conditions.

On this site: [PPO](/docs/foundations/rl-for-robotics/ppo) · [Model predictive control](/docs/foundations/controllers/mpc) · [MicroDuck RL](/docs/practices/humanoid/microduck-rl).

## 6.4 Navigation \{#navigation}

Navigation addresses where a robot is, what surrounds it, and how it can reach a goal. It covers mapping, localization, environmental perception, path planning, obstacle avoidance, and failure recovery.

**Core technologies**:

- **Localization and mapping**: lidar-inertial methods such as FAST-LIO and LIO-SAM, visual-inertial methods such as ORB-SLAM3 and VINS, plus filtering, graph optimization, loop closure, and relocalization.
- **Environmental perception**: camera and lidar calibration, time synchronization, point cloud processing, depth estimation, traversability, and obstacle detection.
- **Planning and navigation execution**: global and local path planning, costmaps, ROS 2, tf2, and [Nav2](https://docs.nav2.org/rolling/). Vision-language navigation (VLN) and semantic navigation additionally involve language understanding and semantic maps.

**Related roles**: navigation engineer, SLAM engineer, localization and mapping engineer, robot perception engineer, and vision-language navigation roles.

On this site: [tf2](/docs/foundations/robotics-and-ros2/tf2) · [Odometry, timestamps, and state estimation](/docs/foundations/perception/odo-timestamps-state-estimation) · [Sensor calibration](/docs/foundations/perception/sensor-calibration-sim2real).

## 6.5 Simulation engineering \{#simulation}

Simulation engineering creates robot environments for training, testing, and reproducible experiments. It supports algorithm development, synthetic data generation, and validation for hardware deployment.

**Core technologies**:

- **Simulation platforms and assets**: Isaac Sim / Isaac Lab, MuJoCo, and scene or robot description formats such as USD, URDF, and MJCF.
- **Physics and sensor modeling**: mass, inertia, friction, collisions, contacts, actuators, and sensors, plus real-to-sim calibration using physical measurements.
- **Experiments at scale**: scene generation, domain randomization, synthetic data, parallel simulation, performance analysis, and automated evaluation. Work involving video world models also requires generative modeling and action-conditioned prediction.

**Related roles**: simulation engineer, robotics simulation developer, synthetic data engineer, and simulation evaluation engineer. Developing world model architectures and training methods usually corresponds to world model ML roles.

On this site: [Simulation overview](/docs/foundations/simulation/intro) · [MuJoCo](/docs/foundations/simulation/mujoco) · [World models](/docs/foundations/world-model/intro).

## 6.6 Data engineering \{#data-engineering}

Data engineering turns teleoperation, robot execution, and simulation data into traceable, trainable, reusable datasets. It connects data collection with model training.

**Core technologies**:

- **Collection and teleoperation**: device interfaces, teleoperation systems, video and robot state recording, and temporal alignment of observations and actions.
- **Data processing**: cleaning, annotation, dropped-frame and anomaly detection, data formats, metadata, and quality checks. Records include the embodiment, coordinate frames, units, control rate, and calibration version.
- **Data platforms**: Python, object storage, job scheduling, version management, dataset splits, high-throughput loading, and robot learning toolchains such as [LeRobot](https://huggingface.co/docs/lerobot/en/index).

**Related roles**: robotics data engineer, data platform engineer, data quality engineer, and teleoperation systems engineer. Data collection operator roles focus more on running collection procedures, operating equipment, and checking collected data.

On this site: [Embodied AI datasets](./resources/datasets.md) · [LeRobot course notes](/docs/practices/robot-arm/data-collection/lerobot-course) · [SO-101 hardware tutorial](/docs/practices/robot-arm/data-collection/so101-lerobot-real).

## 6.7 Deployment engineering \{#deployment}

Deployment engineering connects perception, policies, controllers, and hardware into a reliable running system. It focuses on end-to-end latency, resource use, reliability, and fault recovery.

**Core technologies**:

- **Robot software**: C++, Python, Linux, ROS 2, DDS / QoS, device drivers, multithreading, and interprocess communication.
- **Model inference**: ONNX Runtime, TensorRT, quantization, inference profiling, and the compute, power, and cooling constraints of edge platforms such as Jetson Orin.
- **Operation and maintenance**: real-time scheduling, communication and inference timing, health checks, log replay, version management, deployment rollback, and safe stopping.

**Related roles**: robotics software engineer, robotics systems engineer, model deployment engineer, inference optimization engineer, and robot integration engineer.

On this site: [ROS 2 engineering basics](/docs/foundations/robotics-and-ros2/ros2_engineering_basics) · [Controller integration](/docs/foundations/controllers/integration) · [RTC real-time execution](/docs/foundations/vla/rtc_realtime_execution).

## 6.8 Hardware \{#hardware}

Hardware provides computation, sensing, power, and motion. It determines payload, speed, precision, runtime, and reliability. Each part draws on its own technical discipline.

**Core technologies**:

- **Embedded systems**: C / C++, MCUs, RTOSs, interrupts, DMA, CAN / CAN-FD, SPI / I²C, and sensor and actuator interfaces.
- **Motor drives**: field-oriented control (FOC), current / velocity / position loops, encoders, PWM, power devices, and protection circuits.
- **Electronics**: schematics and PCBs, power supplies, signal integrity, electromagnetic compatibility, thermal design, and board-level testing.
- **Mechanics and actuators**: CAD, mechanisms, materials and tolerances, finite element analysis, motor and gearbox selection, transmissions, assembly, and reliability testing. Dexterous hands also involve tendon or linkage transmissions and tactile sensor integration.

**Related roles**: embedded systems engineer, motor drives engineer, electronics engineer, mechanical engineer, actuator engineer, and dexterous-hand hardware engineer.

On this site: [CAN and MCU communication](/docs/foundations/communication/can-mcu) · [URDF and robot models](/docs/foundations/robotics-and-ros2/urdf_and_rsp). The mechanical design topic is currently a [placeholder](/docs/foundations/hardware/placeholder).
